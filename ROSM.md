

An authentic retro operating system landing page collection built with Eleventy (11ty), featuring pixel-perfect recreations of classic OS interfaces from the 1990s.


The history of operating systems (OS) from 1980 until now covers a **huge range** of systems: personal computers, servers, mainframes, and mobile devices. Below is a **chronological summary** (grouped by family/type) of major operating systems released or widely used from 1980 to today.

---

## 🧠 **1980s – The Early Personal Computer Era**

### **Home/Personal Computers**

* **CP/M (Control Program for Microcomputers)** – pre-1980s but influential through early ’80s
* **Apple DOS 3.x (1978–1983)**
* **Apple ProDOS (1983)**
* **Apple Lisa OS (1983)**
* **Mac OS (System 1) (1984)** – start of Macintosh line
* **MS-DOS (1981)** – became dominant on IBM PCs
* **PC DOS (1981)** – IBM’s version of MS-DOS
* **Atari DOS / TOS (1980s)**
* **Commodore BASIC / GEOS (C64, 1980s)**
* **AmigaOS (1985)**
* **ZX Spectrum BASIC / Sinclair QDOS (1984)**

### **Unix and Derivatives**

* **UNIX System III (1982)**
* **UNIX System V (1983)**
* **BSD 4.1 / 4.2 / 4.3 (1981–1986)**
* **SunOS (1982)**
* **HP-UX (1984)**
* **AIX (1986)**

---

## 💻 **1990s – The GUI and Networking Revolution**

### **Microsoft**

* **Windows 3.0 (1990)**
* **Windows 3.1 (1992)**
* **Windows 95 (1995)**
* **Windows 98 (1998)**
* **Windows NT 3.1 (1993)**
* **Windows NT 4.0 (1996)**

### **Apple**

* **System 7 (1991)**
* **Mac OS 8 (1997)**
* **Mac OS 9 (1999)**

### **UNIX and UNIX-like**

* **Solaris (1992)**
* **IRIX (SGI, 1990)**
* **AIX 4 (1994)**
* **FreeBSD (1993)**
* **NetBSD (1993)**
* **OpenBSD (1996)**

### **Linux**

* **Linux Kernel (1991)** → distributions:

  * **Slackware (1993)**
  * **Debian (1993)**
  * **Red Hat (1994)**
  * **SUSE (1994)**
  * **Mandrake (1998)**

### **Others**

* **BeOS (1995)**
* **OS/2 (1987–2001)** continued to evolve through the 1990s
* **NeXTSTEP (1989, but popularized early 1990s)**
